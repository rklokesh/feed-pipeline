SECTION 1
General Financial Rules (GFR) and VVN
Compliance
Q1. Are books covered under the definition of
“Goods” under GFR?
Books, publications and periodicals for a library
are specifically excluded from the definition of
“Goods” under Rule 143.
Q2. Does the definition of Goods include services
also?
Yes, services incidental or consequential to supply
of goods, such as transportation, insurance,
installation, commissioning, training and
maintenance, are included.
Q3. Can we mention a specific brand in the
GeM/tender specification if that brand is
considered better?
As a general principle, specifications should not
indicate a particular trade mark, trade name or
brand. Specifications should be objective,
functional, generic and measurable.

Q4. Can we prescribe very specific specifications to
get exactly the product required by the school?
Specifications should meet the basic requirement
but should not include superfluous or non-
essential features that may lead to unwarranted
expenditure or restrict competition.
Q5. Can specifications be based on a standard other
than the recognized national standard?
National technical regulations/recognized national
standards should be followed wherever practicable.
A different specification may be adopted only for
reasons recorded in writing.
Q6. Can a school purchase more quantity now
because the item may be required in future?
Excess procurement should be avoided. The Rules
specifically caution against purchasing quantities
in excess of actual requirements because of
inventory carrying costs.
Q7. If an item is available on GeM, can it be
purchased from a local market because the local
supplier is cheaper?
The GFR Rule 149 states that procurement of
Goods and Services available on GeM is mandatory
through GeM.

Q8. For purchases up to ₹50,000 on GeM, is
comparison among multiple sellers mandatory?
Under the provision reproduced in the uploaded
material, purchases up to ₹50,000 may be made
through any available GeM supplier meeting the
requisite quality, specification and delivery
requirements.
Q9. For purchases above ₹50,000 and up to ₹10
lakh, can any GeM seller be selected?
No. The provision requires selection of the lowest-
price seller amongst available sellers of at least
three different manufacturers, subject to quality,
specification and delivery requirements.
Q10. If the same requirement is divided into two or
three smaller purchases, is it permissible?
No. Demand should not be divided into small
quantities to avoid L-1 buying, bidding/reverse
auction or the sanction of a higher authority.
Q11. Is a three-member committee mandatory for
LPC purchases?
Yes. GFR Rule 155 specifically provides for a duly
constituted Local Purchase Committee consisting
of three members.

Q12. Is market survey by the LPC mandatory?
Yes. The LPC is required to survey the market to
ascertain reasonableness of rate, quality and
specifications and identify an appropriate supplier.
Q13. Does LPC recommendation alone complete the
procurement?
No. The Committee recommends the purchase
after carrying out the prescribed assessment. The
purchaser must still follow the applicable financial
powers and approval requirements.
Q14. What certificate should the LPC give?
The members jointly certify that the goods meet the
requisite specification and quality, are priced at the
prevailing market rate, and that the supplier is
reliable and competent and is not debarred.
Q15. Can the ₹50,000 limit be used repeatedly for
the same requirement?
No. Demand should not be split into smaller
purchases merely to avoid the prescribed
procurement procedure or higher sanction.

Q16. If only one bidder participates in an
advertised/limited tender, does it
automatically mean the tender has failed?
Not necessarily. The GFR Rule 173(xix) states that
lack of competition should not be determined solely
by the number of bidders. Even a single bid may be
considered valid if the tender was properly
advertised, sufficient time was given, qualification
criteria were not unduly restrictive and the price is
reasonable.
Q17. Can a technically qualified bidder's financial bid
be opened even if some technical conditions are
not met?
No. Under the two-bid system, financial bids are to
be opened only for technically acceptable offers.
Q18. Can a new eligibility condition be introduced
while evaluating bids?
No. Bids must be evaluated according to the
conditions already incorporated in the bidding
documents. A new condition should not be
introduced at the evaluation stage.

Q19. Can a bidder modify its bid after the deadline
for submission?
No. Bidders should not be permitted to alter or
modify their bids after expiry of the deadline for
receipt of bids.
Q20. Can negotiation be conducted with the lowest
bidder after opening of bids?
Negotiation after bid opening is to be severely
discouraged. It may be resorted to only in
exceptional circumstances and with the lowest
evaluated responsive bidder.
Q21. What are the tender limits for works?
GFR 139 deals with works. Limited tender applies
to works below Rs. 10 lakh; open tender applies
from Rs. 10 lakh to Rs. 60 lakh. Funds should be
deposited with the construction agency on time,
and proper records and accounting maintained
